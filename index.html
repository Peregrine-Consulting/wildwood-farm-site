What makes the model different

Queer-normative care is not an add-on.

Many treatment settings say they are LGBTQ+ friendly. Wildwood’s premise goes further: the program is designed so queer identity is not treated as a complication to work around. It is part of the clinical reality, the community context, and the recovery plan.

That distinction matters for clients carrying minority stress, religious trauma, internalized shame, family rupture, or the fear that sobriety will cost them their community.

Wildwood Invitational Family Intervention

Family work that maps trauma and resilience.

The Wi-Fi model brings family systems into weekly conversations. The goal is not blame. It is a clearer map of what the family has survived, where patterns formed, and how the client’s recovery can be supported after residential care.

Clinical leadership

Dr. Michael Hinkley

Dr. Hinkley’s work is shaped by clinical practice, theology, and two decades as a Catholic priest in inner-city ministry. That background gives Wildwood a distinctive ethic of care for people whose recovery is entangled with shame, faith, family, and identity.

The website should make that story legible without making it self-important: experience, humility, and a clear explanation of why queer-normative treatment changes what clients are able to risk, say, and rebuild.
