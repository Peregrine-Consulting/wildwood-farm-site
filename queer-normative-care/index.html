Our approach

What queer-normative recovery actually means.

An LGBTQ+ friendly program accepts you. That is the floor, and it is not the same thing as being built for you.

Friendly is not the same as built for you

An LGBTQ+ friendly program has a non-discrimination policy and staff who try not to say the wrong thing. That matters, and it is the minimum.

Queer-normative means the program was built around queer experience from the beginning. Your identity is the starting point of the clinical work rather than a complication to be managed. Minority stress, family rupture, and the particular weight of having been unsafe as a young person are treated as part of the picture, because for most people here, they are the picture.

In practice it is quieter than it sounds. Nobody spends the first ten minutes of group explaining themselves. Pronouns are the baseline. You can talk about your relationships without translating them.

How to tell which one you are looking at

These are worth asking on any tour, here or anywhere else:

  • Who designed the clinical programme, and what is their experience with queer clients specifically?
  • Is identity work part of the treatment plan, or handled only if a resident raises it?
  • How are rooms, bathrooms and intake forms handled for transgender and non-binary residents?
  • What happens when another resident says something harmful? Whose job is it to address it?
  • How many people currently in the programme are queer? Would I be the only one?

A program built for you will have ready answers. A program that is merely friendly will improvise.

What it changes day to day

Group work is where the difference is most obvious. When everyone in the room shares the baseline experience of having been treated as an exception, the conversation starts several steps further along. Less explaining, more work.

It also changes what counts as progress. Recovery here is not only abstinence. It is the slower business of no longer organising your life around being acceptable to other people.
