The farm

A landmark farm, and a peaceful oasis to call home.

Wildwood brings together the affirmation of a supportive sober house with the natural beauty of land that has been cared for continuously for more than two centuries.

Two hundred years of the same land

For over two hundred years, Wildwood was a small working farm held by a single family. The barns, the stone walls and the mature trees were all here long before any of us, and the place carries that continuity in a way you feel rather than read about.

That history is part of why it works. Nothing here was built to look like a treatment centre. It looks like what it is: a farm that has been loved and worked for a very long time, and now offers the same shelter to people who need it.

The shoreline, and the villages either side

The farm sits centrally between the beautiful villages of Madison and Guilford on the Connecticut shoreline. Town greens, independent shops and quiet coffee places are minutes away. So are the beaches, horseback riding, and the cultural life of the shoreline and nearby New Haven.

It matters that the farm is peaceful without being isolated. Residents build a real week here, in a real place, with somewhere to walk to.

Space, and what it does for a person

There is room here to be outside without being watched. Open fields, wooded edges, a long view, and the ordinary business of a working farm going on around you. For people who have spent years in small rooms and difficult situations, the sheer scale of that is often the first thing they mention.

Rooms indoors are beautiful and comfortably decorated, because the setting you recover in should feel like somewhere you would choose to be. The peace offered by the land, the animals and the gardens is not decoration; it is part of the clinical value of the place.

Gardens, and the table

Herb and vegetable gardens are worked through the season, and much of what they produce arrives at dinner. The farm-to-table culinary programme means meals are grown, cooked and eaten together, which teaches more about care and routine than any handout on nutrition could.

The animals

Wildwood is a working farm, and the animals are part of daily life rather than a feature of it. A flock of laying hens, the goats, the lambs and pigs, and the therapy dogs who tend to find whoever is having the hardest day.

A place for reflection

Wildwood is a very special place for personal reflection and healing, and residents describe the same things again and again: the light, the quiet, the animals, and the relief of somewhere that expects nothing of them but honesty.
