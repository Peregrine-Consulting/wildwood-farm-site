The program

Six months, on a living farm.

An extended sober living program that is clinically and trauma informed, with leadership on site. Wildwood often serves people who have repeatedly tried other, shorter programs.

Some of this may sound like you

  • You've been through treatment before, and something about it never quite fit.
  • You're sober, or nearly, and going back to your old routine feels like a risk.
  • You want somewhere you can be yourself without explaining yourself.
  • Getting sober feels like it might mean losing the community that took you in.
  • You're worried about someone you love and don't know where to begin.
  • You're not ready to call anyone yet. You're just reading.

Any of those is a reasonable place to start a conversation. You don't have to be certain. You only have to be willing to talk.

What the six months are for

Most relapse happens in the first year after treatment, in the gap where nobody is looking. Twenty-eight days can interrupt a crisis. It rarely builds a lasting sober life.

Six months is long enough to get through the part where everything feels raw, into the part where a routine starts to feel like yours. Long enough to find work, repair some relationships, and practice the day-to-day business of living a sober life more than once.

What a week looks like

Clinical work

The clinical work does not happen here. Wildwood enjoys a number of professional partners — among them Yale New Haven Hospital, Middlesex Health and other local clinical groups — and PHP and IOP care is delivered through them. Being minutes from those centers is deliberate: it means residents can be supported through gender-affirming care, depression, bipolar disorder and dual diagnosis by clinicians who specialize in it.

With those professional resources, each resident gets a program built around them rather than a standard protocol. Group work here is built around queer experience rather than adapted for it, and leadership lives in the same place you do.

The farm

Mornings can begin outside, with the hens, the goats and Macchiato, the farm's dog, and the gardens are tended through the season. Much of what grows here arrives at dinner, which is grown, cooked and eaten together. More about the farm →

Life skills

Addiction takes a great deal out of a life, and relearning the practical skills is essential to recovery. Money, time, employment, cooking — the scaffolding that tends to be missing when someone has been unwell for a long time, relearned here with the goal of an independent life.

Family work

The Wildwood Invitational Family Intervention (Wi-Fi) brings families into the weekly work in a structured way, mapping three generations of what a family has survived — properly acknowledging the trauma, and celebrating the resilience that carried them through it. More for families →

Questions people actually ask

How do I know whether Wildwood is the right program for me?

That is what the first conversation is for. We talk through where you are, what has already been tried, and what kind of support would actually help. If Wildwood isn't the right fit, we would rather tell you that and point you toward something better suited.

Will I lose my community if I get sober?

You will probably lose relationships that were built around drinking and using, and that is a real loss worth grieving. What people find here is a community where they are known fully and don't have to perform anything. For a lot of people those turn out to be the deepest friendships they've had, because they're built on honesty rather than a shared substance.

Is Wildwood only for gay men?

No. Wildwood serves the whole LGBTQ+ community, including transgender and non-binary residents. Pronouns here are the baseline, not a courtesy that has to be requested.

Is it worth relocating to Connecticut for this?

There is no geographic cure, and moving is not the plan by itself. Coming to Wildwood for an extended stay is one part of your recovery — what matters is engaging the program once you are here. It genuinely helps when your current environment is the trigger, when your whole social network is built around drinking and using, or when the program you need doesn't exist where you live. Coming here is about entering a queer-normative program built for you, rather than simply relocating. We'll tell you honestly if we think staying put is the better call.

What if I'm not ready to commit to six months?

Then say that on the call. Six months is what the model is built around and we'll explain why, but the first conversation is about whether this is the right fit at all, including the possibility that it isn't, and that somewhere else is.
