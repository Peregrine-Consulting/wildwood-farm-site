The program

Six months, on a working farm.

Extended sober living with clinical leadership on site, built for people who have usually tried something shorter first.

Some of this may sound like you

  • You've been through treatment before, and something about it never quite fit.
  • You're sober, or nearly, and going back to your old routine feels like a risk.
  • You want somewhere you can be yourself without explaining yourself.
  • Getting sober feels like it might mean losing the community that took you in.
  • You're worried about someone you love and don't know where to begin.
  • You're not ready to call anyone yet. You're just reading.

Any of those is a reasonable place to start a conversation. You don't have to be certain. You only have to be willing to talk.

What the six months are for

Most relapse happens in the first year after treatment, in the gap where nobody is looking. Twenty-eight days can interrupt a crisis. It rarely builds a life that holds afterward.

Six months is long enough to get through the part where everything feels raw, into the part where a routine starts to feel like yours. Long enough to find work, repair some relationships, and practise the ordinary business of a sober week more than once.

What a week looks like

Clinical work

Weekly individual therapy with a licensed clinician, plus group work built around queer experience rather than adapted for it. Most sober living has nobody on site with a graduate degree. Here the clinical leadership lives in the same place you do.

The farm

Mornings begin outside, with the hens, the goats and the dogs, and the gardens are tended through the season. Much of what grows here arrives at dinner, which is grown, cooked and eaten together. More about the farm →

Life skills

Money, time, employment, cooking. The practical scaffolding that tends to be missing when someone has been unwell for a long time.

Family work

The Wildwood Invitational Family Intervention brings families into the weekly work in a structured way. More for families →

Questions people actually ask

How do I know whether this is the right program for me?

That is what the first conversation is for. We talk through where you are, what has already been tried, and what kind of support would actually help. If Wildwood isn't the right fit, we would rather tell you that and point you toward something better suited.

Will I lose my community if I get sober?

You will probably lose relationships that were built around using, and that is a real loss worth grieving. What people find here is a community where they are known fully and don't have to perform anything. For a lot of people those turn out to be the deepest friendships they've had, because they're built on honesty rather than a shared substance.

Is Wildwood only for gay men?

No. Wildwood serves the whole community, including transgender and non-binary residents. Pronouns here are the baseline, not a courtesy that has to be requested.

Is it worth relocating to Connecticut for this?

Sometimes. It genuinely helps when your current environment is the trigger, when your whole social network is built around using, or when the programme you need doesn't exist where you live. It works less well when the move itself becomes the plan. We'll tell you honestly if we think staying put is the better call.

What if I'm not ready to commit to six months?

Then say that on the call. Six months is what the model is built around and we'll explain why, but the first conversation is about whether this is the right fit at all, including the possibility that it isn't, and that somewhere else is.
